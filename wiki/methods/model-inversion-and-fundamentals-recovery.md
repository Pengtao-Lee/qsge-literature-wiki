# Model Inversion and Fundamentals Recovery

Model inversion is the procedure by which a quantitative spatial or trade model is "run backwards": its equilibrium conditions are solved from observed endogenous variables — wages, employment, trade shares, commuting flows, land rents, and population — to recover the unobserved exogenous fundamentals — productivity, amenities, and sometimes trade or commuting costs or match-quality parameters — that rationalise the data as an equilibrium of the model. When the inversion is exact and unique, it converts an untestable assignment of fundamentals into a deterministic mapping from data plus parameters to fundamentals. This recovered set of fundamentals is the essential input for structural estimation of the model's parameters and for counterfactual analysis that conditions on the observed initial equilibrium. The technique is the epistemological twin of exact-hat algebra: inversion makes fundamentals observable, while hat algebra makes them unnecessary for counterfactuals.

## The Identification Problem

Every quantitative spatial model in this literature starts from a decomposition problem: observed outcomes are equilibrium responses to unobserved locational characteristics, and those characteristics are themselves a mix of exogenous fundamentals and endogenous externalities. [[ahlfeldt-redding-sturm-wolf-2015-economics-density]] states the challenge directly: high land prices and concentrated activity are consistent with strong agglomeration forces, but equally with shared amenities or common natural advantages. [[redding-rossi-hansberg-2017-quantitative-spatial]] frames the issue at the level of the model class: quantitative spatial models are typically exactly identified, with a one-to-one mapping from the observed endogenous variables (employment and wages) to the exogenous primitives (productivity and amenities), so a model can always be constructed that rationalises any observed spatial distribution. [[redding-2016-goods-trade-factor-mobility-welfare]] adds the labour-market dimension: with heterogeneous worker preferences, each location faces an upward-sloping supply of population, expected utility is equalised across locations while real incomes differ, and the mapping from location characteristics to populations and wages remains one-to-one. [[allen-arkolakis-2014-trade-topography]] formalises the ambiguity in continuous space, where overall productivity and amenities each combine a fundamental component with endogenous spillovers that depend on the surrounding density of activity; the same observed schedules can be generated by many such decompositions. The identification problem therefore has two halves: recovering fundamentals given parameters, and separating the two using independent variation. As [[redding-rossi-hansberg-2017-quantitative-spatial]] notes, observed wages and populations cannot by themselves identify the structural parameters, because any change in a parameter can be offset by a change in the recovered fundamentals.

## The Inversion Theorem: Exact Recovery of Fundamentals

The core theoretical result is that, given parameters and data on the endogenous variables, the unobserved fundamentals are uniquely determined up to a normalization. [[redding-rossi-hansberg-2017-quantitative-spatial]] demonstrates the theorem for a canonical new economic geography model: inverting the model uses its equilibrium equations to solve for the quality-adjusted land supplies $\{H_n\}$ and productivities $\{A_n\}$ from observed populations $\{L_n\}$ and wages $\{w_n\}$ — the reverse of equilibrium, which determines $\{L_n, w_n\}$ from $\{A_n, H_n\}$. [[redding-2016-goods-trade-factor-mobility-welfare]] proves the analogous proposition for a Ricardian model with heterogeneous preferences: given parameters, a parameterisation of bilateral trade costs, and data on populations, wages, and land supplies, there exist unique values of amenities $\{B_n\}$ and productivities $\{A_n\}$ consistent with the data, up to a choice of units; the proof is recursive — income-equals-expenditure and trade shares give productivities, land market clearing gives rents, the price index gives prices, and residential choice probabilities give amenities. [[allen-arkolakis-2014-trade-topography]] establishes the continuous-space version: for any continuous population and wage schedules there exists a unique (to-scale) pair of productivity and amenity functions that make those schedules a regular spatial equilibrium. [[monte-redding-rossi-hansberg-2018-commuting]] states the applied version for a model with both trade and commuting: given observed data on wages, employment by workplace, commuting flows, and land area, and a parameterisation of trade and commuting costs, the model recovers unique values of the unobserved productivity and amenities that exactly rationalise the data as an equilibrium. The inversion therefore supplies a deterministic mapping from data plus parameters to fundamentals, and the same arguments apply whether the researcher observes trade costs and infers trade shares or observes bilateral trade shares and recovers the costs.

## Conditions for Uniqueness and Invertibility

Inversion inherits its properties from equilibrium uniqueness, because a model with multiple equilibria could in principle rationalise the same data with different fundamentals. [[allen-arkolakis-2014-trade-topography]] provides the sharpest statement for continuous space: writing the dispersion-and-agglomeration exponents as $\gamma_1 \equiv 1 - \alpha(\sigma-1) - \beta\sigma$ and $\gamma_2 \equiv 1 + \alpha\sigma + (\sigma-1)\beta$, a regular equilibrium exists only when $\gamma_1 \neq 0$; when $\gamma_1 > 0$ all equilibria are regular; and the equilibrium is unique when $\gamma_2/\gamma_1 \in [-1, 1]$, in which case it can be computed by a simple iterative procedure. When $\gamma_1 < 0$, no equilibrium is point-wise locally stable. [[redding-rossi-hansberg-2017-quantitative-spatial]] shows that in its discrete new economic geography example the same logic reduces to $\sigma(1-\alpha) > 1$, which guarantees $\gamma_2/\gamma_1 \in (0,1)$ and that dispersion forces dominate agglomeration forces, so the inversion recovering $\{A_n, H_n\}$ has a unique solution. [[redding-2016-goods-trade-factor-mobility-welfare]] obtains the corresponding condition $\sigma(1-\tilde{\alpha}) > 1$ with worker heterogeneity, which specialises to Helpman's condition $\sigma(1-\alpha) > 1$ in the no-heterogeneity limit. The role of regular equilibria matters for the empirical strategy: [[ahlfeldt-redding-sturm-wolf-2015-economics-density]] notes that once agglomeration forces are present multiple equilibria are possible, but the mapping from data and parameters to structural residuals remains unique regardless, because the equilibrium conditions contain enough information to solve for the residuals even when the model is not globally concave.

> **Current assessment (2026-07):** The regularity condition appears under different notation — $\gamma_1 > 0$ in Allen–Arkolakis and $\sigma(1-\alpha) > 1$ in the Redding–Rossi-Hansberg survey — but it is the same economic object: dispersion forces (inelastic land, commuting costs) must dominate agglomeration forces for the inversion to be well-defined and the counterfactual determinate.

## Empirical Implementation

Applied implementations differ in which fundamentals are recovered and from which data. [[monte-redding-rossi-hansberg-2018-commuting]] inverts a trade-and-commuting model for US counties: using the equality between income and expenditure, county trade deficits $D_i$ allocated from the Commodity Flow Survey, observed wages, employment, average residential income, and residents, the model yields a system of $N$ equations in $N$ unobserved county productivities,

$$w_i L_i - \sum_{n \in N} \frac{L_i \left(d_{ni} w_i / A_i\right)^{1-\sigma}}{\sum_{k \in N} L_k \left(d_{nk} w_k / A_k\right)^{1-\sigma}} \left[\bar{v}_n R_n + D_n\right] = 0,$$

which is solved for a unique vector of productivities; the commuting model is then inverted to recover the "ease of commuting" $\mathcal{B}_{ni} \equiv B_{ni}\kappa_{ni}^{-\varepsilon}$ for every pair, with the distance coefficient estimated at $-4.43$ against $-1.29$ for goods trade and the Fréchet heterogeneity parameter at $\varepsilon = 3.30$. [[ahlfeldt-redding-sturm-wolf-2015-economics-density]] performs the block-level analogue for Berlin, recovering adjusted overall productivity, amenities, and density of development from floor prices, wages, residence employment, and commuting market access, without taking a stand on the relative importance of externalities versus fundamentals; the commuting semi-elasticity is estimated at $\nu = \varepsilon\kappa = 0.07$ and the Fréchet parameter at $\varepsilon = 6.83$. [[redding-rossi-hansberg-2017-quantitative-spatial]] provides a worked illustration of the same procedure on a $30 \times 30$ grid, in which adding population data for a region would immediately yield estimates of its productivity. [[tsivanidis-2026-urban-transit-infrastructure]] shows that the commuting market access terms of an urban model can themselves be recovered uniquely (to scale) from residential populations, employment, commute costs $d_{ij} = \exp(\kappa t_{ij})$, and the commuting elasticity. All three need the levels of endogenous variables — heavier data than [[exact-hat-algebra]] counterfactuals, which require only initial-equilibrium values.

## Inversion as Input to Parameter Estimation

Because inversion recovers fundamentals conditional on parameters, it is naturally embedded in a two-step estimation strategy: invert to obtain structural residuals, then estimate parameters from moment conditions in those residuals. [[ahlfeldt-redding-sturm-wolf-2015-economics-density]] is the canonical implementation. Given parameters, adjusted production and residential fundamentals are closed-form functions of observed data and parameters — the structural residuals — and the paper estimates the agglomeration and commuting parameters by efficient GMM on moment conditions that impose that changes in these residuals are uncorrelated with the exogenous change in the surrounding concentration of activity induced by Berlin's division and reunification. The central estimates are an elasticity of productivity with respect to workplace employment density of about 0.07 and an elasticity of amenities with respect to residence density of about 0.15, both highly localised. [[redding-rossi-hansberg-2017-quantitative-spatial]] describes the same division of labour abstractly: recovered fundamentals let the observed variation in the data be decomposed within the model into the contributions of each fundamental, and additional data not used in the quantification, or exogenous shocks, provide the overidentification checks that identify parameters. [[monte-redding-rossi-hansberg-2018-commuting]] estimates the commuting and trade elasticities from gravity equations first and then recovers fundamentals, while [[tsivanidis-2026-urban-transit-infrastructure]] estimates spillover parameters by GMM from model-inferred productivities and amenities, with TransMilenio providing the exogenous shock to commuting market access that identifies the residential supply elasticity and the productivity and amenity spillovers.

## Recovering Match-Quality and Network Fundamentals

The inversion logic extends beyond productivity and amenities to the match and network objects that microfound spatial choice. [[tsivanidis-2026-urban-transit-infrastructure]] models each worker's productivity with each potential employer as a draw from a location-specific Fréchet distribution $F(\varepsilon_j) = \exp(-\tilde{T}_g \varepsilon_j^{-\theta_g})$, where $\theta_g$ controls the dispersion of match productivities and $\tilde{T}_g$ their level; recovering these match-productivity fundamentals (with $\theta_H = 2.44 < \theta_L = 4$ for high- and low-skill workers, and $T_g$ calibrated to the college wage premium) is the same operation as recovering productivity in a trade model, and the structural residual in the reduced form reflects changing exogenous location fundamentals — productivities, amenities, and floorspace supplies. [[ahlfeldt-redding-sturm-wolf-2015-economics-density]] recovers commuting market access, a network object that summarises access to jobs, from the labour market clearing condition before productivity and amenities are solved. [[monte-redding-rossi-hansberg-2018-commuting]] treats the ease of commuting as the network fundamental recovered from observed commuting flows, and [[redding-rossi-hansberg-2017-quantitative-spatial]] shows that if bilateral trade shares rather than trade costs are observed, then trade costs can also be recovered alongside productivities and land supplies. These recovered network objects are themselves the subject of dedicated measurement (see [[commuter-market-access]]).

## What Inversion Can and Cannot Test

The defining epistemic caveat is that an inverted model's ability to explain the data is not a test of the model. [[redding-rossi-hansberg-2017-quantitative-spatial]] is explicit: because the model is exactly identified, the unobserved fundamentals are free parameters that can be adjusted to ensure the model exactly matches the data, so the model's fit cannot be used as evidence in its favour — the recovered fundamentals account for the variation in the data but do not explain it. Inversion tests only whether *some* fundamentals can rationalise the observed outcomes, not whether the model's mechanisms are correct. Breaking the circularity requires independent variation. [[ahlfeldt-redding-sturm-wolf-2015-economics-density]] provides the cleanest example: a model with exogenous productivity and amenities, inverted to fit pre-war Berlin, is used to predict the counterfactual treatment effects of division; because those predicted effects are substantially smaller than the actual ones, the exogenous-fundamentals model is rejected, and the exogenous variation of the Berlin Wall validates the estimated agglomeration mechanisms. [[allen-arkolakis-2014-trade-topography]] and [[redding-2016-goods-trade-factor-mobility-welfare]] make the same point formally: without overidentification checks or natural experiments, the same data can be rationalised by many parameterisations, and different models imply different recovered fundamentals even when they fit identically.

*Across the corpus, inversion alone never validates a model: validation comes from out-of-sample predictions or natural experiments, of which the Berlin Wall is the cleanest.*

The relationship to the other tools of the toolkit follows directly. Inversion recovers the unobserved fundamentals that make the data an equilibrium; [[exact-hat-algebra]] then cancels those fundamentals in counterfactual computation, so they need never be solved; and the [[sufficient-statistics-in-spatial-models]] of the welfare literature summarise counterfactual welfare without any reference to the fundamentals at all. The three are complementary answers to the same identification problem: make the unobservables observable, render them irrelevant, or summarise the outcome that does not depend on them.

## Open Questions

The static, single-sector setting in which inversion is best understood does not yet cover the frontier of the literature. [[redding-rossi-hansberg-2017-quantitative-spatial]] identifies the open cases: models with firm entry, physical capital, or dynamics break the one-to-one mapping, and the curse of dimensionality grows with the number of locations, so that exact inversion becomes computationally demanding. [[kleinman-liu-redding-2023-dynamic-spatial]] develops a dynamic spatial model in which the steady state is unique under a spectral-radius condition and the transition path can be characterised analytically, but the inversion of such dynamic models — recovering fundamentals that rationalise an observed transition — is considerably harder than the static case. [[desmet-nagy-rossi-hansberg-2018-geography-development]] embeds the same dynamics through innovation and technology diffusion. Identification of parameters is also fragile when the GMM objective is not globally concave: [[ahlfeldt-redding-sturm-wolf-2015-economics-density]] shows by grid search and Monte Carlo that its particular objective is well behaved, but notes this is a property to be verified case by case rather than assumed.

> **Current assessment (2026-07):** Exact inversion is fully worked out for static gravity-class models; the frontier is inversion under dynamics (Kleinman–Liu–Redding), endogenous capital, and many-location settings where the mapping is no longer one-to-one or globally concave.
